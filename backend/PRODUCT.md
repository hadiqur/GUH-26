# Product

## Register

product

## Users
People who chose a dumbphone on purpose (digital minimalists) and use Brick by calling one number from any phone, even a 2005 Nokia. Second audience: older people and anyone who finds typing on a keypad hard. They open the web dashboard rarely, on a laptop, to set up contacts and preferences; during a call they never see it. At the hackathon, judges see it on a projector while the caller talks to Brick live.

## Product Purpose
Brick is an AI assistant you reach by phone call. It sends email from your own Gmail, texts contacts, sets reminders that text you later, and reads unread email aloud. The dashboard is the quiet control room behind the call: who your contacts are, which reminders are queued, and how Brick should sign off. Success: a caller sets something up once here, then never needs a screen again, and anything done by voice appears here live.

## Brand Personality
Precise, calm, tactile. Brick feels like a well-made piece of hardware (an instrument, not an app): it does a few things exactly and gets out of the way. Futuristic in the way good industrial design is futuristic, not in the sci-fi-neon sense. Voice is plain British English, short, specific.

## Anti-references
- Generic AI SaaS dashboards: soft rounded cards, blue primary buttons, hero metrics, gradient accents.
- Sci-fi neon dark mode with glowing cyan and glassmorphism.
- Anything that looks like a smartphone app competing for attention (badges, feeds, notification dots everywhere).

## Design Principles
1. The phone number is the product. The call-in number is the hero, always visible and dialable.
2. Practice what we preach: no distractions, no decoration that doesn't carry state.
3. Hardware honesty: controls look and behave like physical keys and readouts; one signal colour marks what's live or queued.
4. Voice and screen are one system: changes made on a call show up here immediately and visibly.
5. Readable by anyone: large type, strong contrast, big targets, keyboard-first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA minimum. Older users: base text ≥18px, body contrast ≥7:1 where possible, 44px+ targets, visible focus. Respect reduced motion. Never rely on colour alone for status (pair with text and glyphs). Dot-matrix display type is only used at large sizes, never for body copy.
